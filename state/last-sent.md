Google has added three reporting options to the tool that pulls Search Console data into your own dashboards.

*🟠 Google adds branded and image-search data to its API*
_Google Search Central Blog · November 16, 2026_

*Synopsis*
Google's Search Console team announced that three reports you could already see in Search Console can now be pulled automatically through the Search Analytics API.
· Branded versus non-branded search traffic can now be split automatically. Only "eligible properties" get this data; other sites get no rows.
· Visual search (Google Lens, Circle to Search, image uploads) can now be reported on its own. Set the type to webMultimodal.
· One request can now cover several countries or devices at once, such as EMEA, LATAM or DACH.
· Visual search data will also appear in BigQuery for sites that already have bulk data export switched on.
· Google says the three filters work together, so you can combine them in one view.

*What the terms mean*
Search Analytics API — a way for software to pull your Google search performance data automatically, instead of someone downloading it by hand
Search Console — Google's free tool showing how your site performs in Google Search
Branded queries — searches that contain your company or product name
Non-branded queries — searches where people don't know you yet, so they use generic words
Multimodal search — searching with something other than typed words, like a photo or a screen selection
Google Lens — Google's app for searching by pointing your camera at something
Circle to Search — an Android feature where you circle anything on screen to search it
BigQuery bulk data export — an automatic, daily copy of your Search Console data into Google's data warehouse
EMEA, LATAM, DACH — sales-region labels: Europe/Middle East/Africa, Latin America, and Germany/Austria/Switzerland

*Why it matters for your content*
Branded traffic shows people who already know you. Non-branded traffic shows whether your content wins new people. Mixed together, they hide what your content is actually earning. Visual search is now a separate line you can track, so you can see if your product images and pages get found that way.

*Example*
A skincare brand sells in Germany, Austria and Switzerland. Before, its weekly report mixed searches for its own name with searches like "vitamin C serum for sensitive skin". Now one report can cover all three countries, split brand-name searches from generic ones, and show how many visits came from photo-based search on phones.

*Do this*
Ask whoever builds your search reports to add the branded split and the visual search line, and to check first whether your site is an eligible property.
