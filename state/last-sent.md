*Search Console now shows visual search performance*
_Google Search Central Blog · 2026-09-24_

*What happened*
Google added multimodal Search performance reporting to Search Console. It shows how users find your content through visual and multimodal experiences, not just plain text queries.

*What the terms mean*
Multimodal search — search that mixes images, text, or voice, not just typed words.
Search Console — Google's free tool showing how your site performs in Search.

*Why it matters for your content*
Until now, traffic from visual queries was invisible in your reports. This lets you see whether images or visual content are driving discovery, which can justify investment in photography, diagrams, or alt text.

*Example*
A recipe site adds new step-by-step photos. The new report shows traffic arriving from visual queries on those photos — traffic that used to be lumped in as "unknown" or missing entirely.

*Do this*
Open the new multimodal performance report in Search Console this week and see if visual traffic shows up for your site.

*Google rolled out a September spam algorithm update*
_Google Search Status Dashboard · 2026-09-24_

*What happened*
Google released the September 2026 spam update. It applies globally, in all languages. Rollout began 2026-09-24 and may take up to two weeks to finish.

*What the terms mean*
Spam update — an algorithm change targeting low-quality or manipulative content, which can move rankings up or down even if you changed nothing.

*Why it matters for your content*
Rankings may shift over the next two weeks purely from this rollout. If you see a drop, check whether it lines up with the rollout window before assuming a bug or a competitor move.

*Example*
A site with thin, auto-generated blog posts loses rankings by mid-October as the rollout completes, while a competitor with original, well-researched guides gains ground.

*Do this*
Watch Search Console rankings for swings through mid-October before making any strategy changes.

*Google tightens rules on rented site reputation*
_Google Search Central Blog · 2026-08-28_

*What happened*
Google updated its site reputation policy, first introduced in 2024. The policy targets third-party content published on a trusted site solely to borrow that site's ranking power.

*What the terms mean*
Site reputation abuse — letting an outside company publish content on your trusted domain just to exploit your site's authority in Search, e.g. a coupon business running a section under a news site's URL.

*Why it matters for your content*
If you run sponsored, licensed, or partner content in a subfolder or subdomain of your site, those pages are now at higher risk of being excluded from rankings — even if the rest of your site is unaffected.

*Example*
A finance site hosts a "reviews" subfolder fully operated by a third-party affiliate company. That subfolder could get de-indexed under the updated policy, while the finance site's own content keeps ranking.

*Do this*
Audit any third-party-run subfolders or subdomains on your domain and confirm they're not just borrowing your reputation.

https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/08/update-site-reputation-policy
