*🔴 Google adds more inline links in AI answers*
_Google — The Keyword (Search) · October 15, 2026_

*Synopsis*
Google is reshaping AI Overviews and AI Mode so people click out to the open web more, not just read the summary and leave.
· Inline links now appear higher up in AI answers, not just buried at the bottom.
· New dedicated section shows a brand's official website directly inside the answer.
· Visual carousels now surface news articles, plus clickable creator profiles on video results.
· Use of "Preferred Sources" has grown over 7x since the start of 2026.

*What the terms mean*
AI Overviews — the AI-written answer Google puts above the blue links
AI Mode — Google's full chatbot-style search experience, separate from the AI Overviews summary
Inline links — clickable links placed inside the AI-written text itself, not listed separately below it
Preferred Sources — a Search setting letting people pick specific sites they want shown more often

*Why it matters for your content*
More inline links mean more direct paths from an AI answer straight to your page, not just to generic "sources" lists. Pages written so a sentence or claim can be linked mid-answer have a better shot at the click. Being set up as someone's "preferred source" also now carries more weight than before.

*Example*
Someone searches "best espresso machines under $500." Before: your product page sat buried in a sources list under the AI Overview. After: the AI answer links your page inline, mid-sentence, right where it recommends a machine — and a search for your brand name shows a dedicated "official website" box pointing straight to your homepage.

*Do this*
Search your own brand name and check the new "official website" box points to the right page; check whether key product/comparison pages read clearly enough to be worth linking inline.

*🟠 400 publishers now join Google's AI News pilot*
_Google — The Keyword (Search) · October 15, 2026_

*Synopsis*
Google is paying over 400 publications worldwide to license their content for its AI News features, under what it calls the News AI pilot program.
· More than 400 publications now take part, up from the original group (Washington Examiner, Washington Post).
· New partners include Axios, CNN, Financial Times, Forbes, plus local outlets like Texas Tribune and Shaw Media.
· Google pays publishers for content used in AI article overviews on Google News pages and custom audio briefings.
· Features include clear attribution and direct links back to the original publisher.

*What the terms mean*
News AI pilot program — Google's paid licensing deal with publishers for content used in its AI News features
Attribution — clearly crediting the original source of a piece of content

*Why it matters for your content*
This is Google paying for structured, licensed content and crediting the source — a different model from free crawling. It mainly applies to news-style publishers, but it's a signal of where paid content licensing is heading and worth knowing before it comes up in a meeting.

*Example*
A company running an in-house industry newsletter with news-style reporting (like local outlet Oil City News did) could ask whether it qualifies for a similar paid licensing deal with Google.

*Do this*
If you run or work with a newsroom-style publication, ask your PR team to check eligibility for this program. Otherwise, nothing to do yet — just know it's coming.
