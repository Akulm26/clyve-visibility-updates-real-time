*🔴 Study: FAQs, schema don't drive AI citations*
_arXiv — AI search & citation research · September 28, 2026_

*What happened*
Researchers tracked 2 million AI citations across ChatGPT, Claude, Google AI, and Gemini, matched to 10,000 pages from 19 B2B SaaS companies. The biggest predictor of getting cited was how closely a page's wording matched real buyer questions — not FAQ blocks, structured data, or page speed. Whichever domain already had the most AI authority mattered six times more than any single page tweak.

*What the terms mean*
structured data — code added to a page that tells search engines what it's about
AI authority — how much a model already trusts and cites a whole website, not just one page

*Why it matters for your content*
Common checklist advice — FAQ schema, structured data, fast pages — may not be what's earning citations. What matters more is matching your page's language to how buyers actually phrase questions, plus overall site authority built over time. Ticking boxes alone probably won't move the needle.

*Example*
A pricing page has perfect FAQ schema and loads in under a second, but is written in internal jargon. A competitor's plainer, buyer-worded page on a more established domain gets cited instead — even with worse technical setup.

*Do this*
Audit top pages for language match to real buyer questions, not just checklist compliance.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35077v1

*🔴 AI shopping agents skip sites they can't read*
_arXiv — AI search & citation research · September 28, 2026_

*What happened*
A study ran nearly 38,000 simulated buyer journeys across four AI agent tools, covering 1,056 real businesses. Sites easy for an AI agent to fetch and read got worked into the final answer 78% of the time, versus 56% for hard-to-read sites, and were recommended almost twice as often. When an agent could read the site directly, its answer was 41% more accurate.

*What the terms mean*
answer-engine optimization (AEO) — getting AI engines to find and recommend you by scattering mentions across the web (forums, listicles, reviews)
agent experience (AX) — whether an AI agent can actually open and read your own site once it decides to look closer

*Why it matters for your content*
Getting mentioned elsewhere online is no longer the finish line. Once a buyer's AI agent narrows its options, it tries to visit your actual site — and if it can't read it, you lose the citation and the recommendation, even if people already talk about you online.

*Example*
Your brand gets namechecked in ten "best CRM tools" listicles. But your own product pages sit behind a login wall or heavy JavaScript. The agent can't fetch them, so it recommends the competitor whose plain HTML pricing page it could actually open.

*Do this*
Check whether your key pages (pricing, product, comparison) are readable by a simple fetch — no login wall, no JS-only rendering.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34951v1

*🟠 Search Console now reports visual search*
_Google Search Central Blog · September 24, 2026_

*What happened*
Google added a new report in Search Console showing how users find your content through more visual and multimodal search experiences. It's aimed at publishers who want this data separate from standard text search.

*What the terms mean*
Search Console — Google's free tool that shows how your pages perform in Search (clicks, impressions, rankings)
multimodal search — search that mixes text, images, and other formats in one query or answer

*Why it matters for your content*
You'll be able to see how much of your traffic comes from image and visual search rather than plain text. That helps you decide whether to invest in image quality and alt text, or leave it alone.

*Example*
A recipe site notices in the new report that 30% of its clicks come from visual search on its step photos, not the recipe text. It starts optimizing photo quality and captions instead of just the article copy.

*Do this*
Check the new report in Search Console to see your visual-search traffic split.

https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/09/web-multimodal-in-sc

*🟠 Cloudflare lets you block AI training, not search*
_Cloudflare Blog (AI) · September 15, 2026_

*What happened*
Cloudflare launched a way to tell mixed-use AI crawlers apart, so site owners can allow one job and block the other. Until now, blocking AI training often meant losing visibility in AI search too, because the same crawler did both jobs.

*What the terms mean*
AI crawler — automated bot that visits and downloads your pages for an AI company
mixed-use crawler — one crawler used for two jobs (training AI models and powering live AI search), which used to be an all-or-nothing choice

*Why it matters for your content*
Previously, blocking your content from training AI models could also mean disappearing from AI search citations. Now you can keep content out of training data while staying eligible to be cited in AI answers, protecting your content without losing visibility.

*Example*
A publisher blocks a company's training crawler to stop articles feeding model training, but keeps that same company's search-answer crawler allowed — so it still shows up as a cited source in AI answers, without its text training future models.

*Do this*
If you use Cloudflare, review your bot rules and split AI-training crawlers from AI-search crawlers instead of blocking or allowing everything at once.

https://blog.cloudflare.com/accountable-mixed-use-ai-crawlers/
