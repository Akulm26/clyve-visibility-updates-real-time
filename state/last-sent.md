*🔴 Big study: what really gets you cited by AI*
_arXiv — AI search & citation research · September 28, 2026_

*What happened*
Researchers tracked ~2 million citations from ChatGPT, Claude, Google AI, and Gemini over six months, across 10,000 pages from 19 B2B SaaS companies. Two things predicted citations best: how closely a page's wording matched real user questions, and the site's overall reputation with AI — worth 6x more than any single-page trick. Standard AEO tactics (FAQ blocks, structured data, page speed) looked helpful in the raw data, but the effect vanished or reversed once pages were compared within the same site.

*What the terms mean*
AEO — answer engine optimization, tuning pages to get picked and cited by AI answers.
Domain authority — the reputation AI systems seem to give your whole site, not one page.
Simpson's paradox — a pattern that looks true in bulk data but flips once split by group (here, by site).

*Why it matters for your content*
Teams chasing FAQ/schema checklists may be optimizing the wrong layer. Matching real user language and building site-wide trust matters more than page-level tricks. A well-optimized page on a low-authority domain likely won't move citations much.

*Example*
A SaaS company builds a schema-heavy "Best CRM for Startups" page, but their domain is rarely cited elsewhere. Per this study, that page likely underperforms a plainer page on a site AI already trusts, phrased the way people actually ask ("what CRM works for a 5-person startup").

*Do this*
Check whether your citation problem is page-level or domain-level before investing further in FAQ/schema fixes alone.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35077v1

*🔴 AI agents now read your site, not just mentions of it*
_arXiv — AI search & citation research · September 28, 2026_

*What happened*
Researchers ran ~38,000 simulated buyer research journeys across 1,056 real businesses using four different AI agent systems. Sites that were easy for agents to fetch and read ("agent-ready") had their own pages used in answers 78% of the time vs. 56% for others, and were recommended almost twice as often. Answers were 41% more accurate when built from a readable site.

*What the terms mean*
AX — agent experience, how easily an AI agent can fetch and read your site (not how a human visitor experiences it).
Grounded answer — an AI answer actually built from real page content, not from the model's memory.

*Why it matters for your content*
Being mentioned elsewhere (classic AEO) isn't enough — agents now visit and read your own pages before answering. A site that's slow, script-heavy, or blocks crawlers gets skipped even if you're well-known.

*Example*
A prospect asks an AI shopping agent "which project management tool handles freelancers best." If your pricing page is easy to fetch and parse, it gets used directly and you get named. If it's JS-heavy or blocked, the agent falls back to old forum posts about you — or skips you.

*Do this*
Test whether your key pages are actually fetchable and readable by AI agents, not just by Googlebot.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34951v2

Google released its September 2026 spam update, global and all-language, starting September 24, 2026. Rollout can take up to two weeks to fully complete.

*What the terms mean*
Spam update — an algorithm change that targets low-quality or manipulative content in search rankings.

*Why it matters for your content*
Rankings can shift for two weeks while this rolls out. Since many AI Overviews pull from top organic results, pages affected here can also lose AI citation visibility, not just blue-link position.

*Example*
A site running large batches of thin, templated blog posts to catch long-tail queries could see both rankings and AI citations drop mid-rollout, even if nothing on the site changed.

*Do this*

*What the terms mean*
